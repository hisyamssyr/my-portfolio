## Context

I built an end-to-end classifier for handwritten Chinese numerals: 15 classes, 10,500 images, drawn by 70 different writers. The pipeline covers preprocessing, training, evaluation, and diagnostics.

## Problem

Writer style dominates handwriting recognition. If the same writer appears in both training and test data, the model can memorize a person instead of a character — and the reported accuracy lies. I wanted an evaluation protocol that cannot cheat.

## Approach

**Reproducible preprocessing.** One deterministic path from raw images to cached arrays, with disk caching so every experiment starts from identical inputs.

**Augmentation for real variation.** Custom augmentation aimed at handwriting differences — stroke thickness, slant, and position — instead of generic image noise.

**A tuned ResNet-18.** A custom head and training recipe on the ResNet-18 backbone: EMA weights, AdamW, learning-rate scheduling, label smoothing, and mixed-precision training.

**Honest evaluation.** Cross-validation grouped by writer, so no writer's handwriting appears on both sides of a split. Multiple seeds are ensembled, test-time augmentation stabilizes inference, and Grad-CAM shows where the model actually looks.

## What I built

- The full pipeline: preprocessing, training, evaluation, and diagnostics in one reproducible codebase.
- A writer-grouped cross-validation harness with multi-seed ensembling and test-time augmentation.
- Grad-CAM visualizations per class and per error case.
